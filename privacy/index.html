Privacy Policy for Karot

Last updated: 31 October 2026

Which app this policy covers

This policy covers the iPhone app sold on the App Store as Karot. The same app is called Carrot on your Home Screen, inside the app and in iOS Settings. "Karot", "Carrot" and "the app" in this policy all mean that one app (App Store ID 6823929869, bundle ID com.toby.carrot), together with its Screen Time extensions, the Karot relay server described below, and this website. Whichever name you see, this policy applies.

The short version

  • Karot has no account and no sign-in. It has no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third party code. It never asks for your email address or Apple Account, and we cannot link what it sends to either.
  • Almost everything Karot does happens on your iPhone, and we cannot see what it stores there.
  • The one exception is Rewire, the optional programme in the Carrot + Rewire plan. Only if you agree in the app, Rewire sends your sheet photo, your goal, the first name you typed, your affirmation and your recent tied tasks through our relay server to OpenAI, which writes your options and checks your sheet. Each Rewire request also carries security data that proves it comes from a genuine copy of the app with an active plan.
  • Our relay does not store or log what you send or what OpenAI returns. It keeps a few pseudonymous security and usage records for fixed periods, listed below. OpenAI may keep requests for up to 30 days to check for abuse, and does not use them to train its models.
  • Apple handles all payments. We never see your card or your Apple Account.
  • We do not sell or share your data for advertising, and never will.

What stays on your iPhone

Karot stores the following on your iPhone. We cannot see any of it. Apart from the Rewire items listed under "What leaves your iPhone", none of it is sent anywhere. Deleting the app deletes all of it except the Keychain entry described below.

In the app's private storage (an App Group container shared only by the app and its own extensions):

  • Your daily list: the items you type, whether each is ticked, and when.
  • Each day's state: credits earned and spent, whether a reward window is open and when it ends, and your settings (such as window length and whether you have linked a Reminders list).
  • Your plan status, as Apple reports it: which plan you have and when it runs to, so the shield switches off by itself if the plan ends.
  • Your app selection, as opaque tokens (see "Screen Time data" below).
  • Your history: a record of every day you planned, with its items, ticks and tick times, carried-over items, minutes unlocked and the window you chose. The app shows it in the week strip, in past days, on Stats and in your carrot patch. It is kept on your phone until you delete the app. A separate short summary of the last 90 days helps the app roll each day over correctly.
  • Your baseline: if you set one, the number of minutes a day you used your chosen apps before Karot, and when you set it.
  • Your Rewire programme, if you start one: your goal, the first name you typed, your affirmation, a record of each day's tied task and whether you ticked it, and your Sunday belief scores (0 to 10).
  • Safety copies: a backup of your app selection, and copies of some files the app finds damaged, kept so your data can be recovered.

In the app's settings: the date you agreed to Rewire's use of AI, the identifier of the app's App Attest key (see "What leaves your iPhone"), the identifier and name of the Reminders list you linked, and small display facts such as when the last celebration played.

In the iOS Keychain: one entry holding a short log of dates: when you installed the app, any reinstall, days you used the once-a-day bypass, when Screen Time access was withdrawn, when the phone's clock looked wrong, when an early Screen Time event was ignored, and when a Rewire programme started and ended. It holds no list items, no app names and no usage figures, and it is never sent anywhere. See "The Keychain entry" below, because it can outlast the app.

Not stored at all: your sheet photos (held in memory only while they are checked) and your answers to the two check-in questions when you set up Rewire (used on screen, then discarded).

Karot uses no iCloud storage and does not sync between devices.

Screen Time data

Karot uses Apple's Screen Time frameworks. Here is exactly what it can and cannot see.

  • Which apps you shield. You choose them in Apple's own picker. The app receives opaque tokens that only iOS can read. Karot cannot turn them back into app or website names and never knows which apps you chose.
  • When an allowance runs out. While a reward window is open, iOS tells the app when the window's time has been used, as a single event.
  • Your average time in your chosen apps. When you set your baseline, Karot shows your average daily time in the apps you chose, over up to the last 28 days. iOS works this out inside a sealed report extension that Apple runs separately from the rest of the app. That extension cannot connect to the internet or pass the figures back to the app. Karot draws the figure on screen and does not store it or send it anywhere. If you then set a baseline, the number you choose is saved on your phone, as listed above.

Karot does not read your browsing history, and it never sends Screen Time data off your iPhone.

What leaves your iPhone

This is the complete list.

1. Rewire content, only if you use Rewire and have agreed in the app:

  • the photo of your handwritten sheet (taken with the camera, or chosen from your library through Apple's picker, which gives the app only the photo you pick), sent as a resized copy;
  • your goal, as you wrote it;
  • the first name you typed, if any;
  • your affirmation, and the day's seal word the app asks you to write;
  • which week of the programme you are in;
  • the titles of your tied tasks from the last 7 days, and whether you ticked each one.

Your goal is sent as you wrote it. If it mentions your health or another private matter, that is sent too, so you may prefer to phrase it in general terms. Anything visible in your photo is sent with it, so frame only the sheet.

2. Security data, sent with each Rewire request:

  • App Attest data: an identifier for a key that iOS creates for the app on your iPhone, and a signature made with it. Apple's App Attest service uses these to show the request comes from a genuine copy of Karot on a genuine iPhone. When the key is first set up, your iPhone also contacts Apple to certify it.
  • Your Rewire plan's signed transaction: the record Apple gave your iPhone when you subscribed. It includes Apple's transaction identifiers, the plan, its purchase and expiry dates, and details Apple adds such as the store country and price. It contains no name, email address or payment details.

3. Your IP address. Any internet request reveals your IP address. Cloudflare, which runs our relay, uses it to deliver the request. Our relay also uses it, for a minute at a time, to limit how often the key set-up step can be called. We do not store it.

4. What Apple receives. Purchases and subscriptions go through Apple, under Apple's privacy policy. If you have chosen in iOS Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send us anonymous crash reports.

Nothing else leaves your iPhone. Karot sends nothing in the background and nothing when Rewire is off. Apart from tied-task titles, your lists never leave the phone, and nor do your Reminders, Screen Time data, belief scores, check-in answers or Keychain log.

Our relay server

Karot never contacts OpenAI directly. Each Rewire request goes to our relay, a small server on Cloudflare Workers. The relay checks the App Attest data and your plan, applies daily limits, sends the Rewire content to OpenAI, and returns OpenAI's answer to your iPhone.

The relay does not store or log your photo, your text or OpenAI's answer. It does not pass OpenAI your IP address, your App Attest data or your transaction. OpenAI receives only the Rewire content and our instructions.

The relay keeps only these records:

Record What it contains How long
Set-up challenges A random one-time code Up to 5 minutes
Your App Attest key The key's identifier and public half, a counter, whether it is a test or live key, and when it was created and last used Deleted after 183 days (about 6 months) without use
Daily request counts Today's number of Rewire requests of each kind, filed under a one-way hash of your plan's original transaction identifier Deleted after 3 days
Daily spend The total cost of all users' requests that day, as one number 400 days (not personal data)
Operational logs For each request: which relay function was called and the result; for Rewire requests, also the request kind and the first 8 characters of the one-way hash Kept by Cloudflare for up to 7 days

A daily clean-up removes expired records, so each can last up to a day longer than shown. These records are pseudonymous. We cannot connect them to your name, your Apple Account or your device without extra information, and we do not try. We still treat them as personal data.

OpenAI

Rewire uses OpenAI, an AI company, to write your affirmation options and your daily tied task, and to check the photo of your sheet. Requests are made under our OpenAI API account, so OpenAI's business terms and data processing terms apply, not the consumer ChatGPT terms. Under those terms OpenAI does not use the content to train its models, and keeps it for up to 30 days to detect abuse, unless the law requires it to keep it longer.

The sheet check is automated: OpenAI's answer decides whether today's sheet counts. It has no legal or similar effect on you. If you think a check got it wrong, take a new photo and try again. If our checking service has run out of capacity for the day, the app lets the day through rather than locking you out.

Where data leaves the UK or the European Economic Area, it is protected by safeguards approved under UK and EU law, such as the UK International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, or the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified.

Your choices

  • Withdraw your AI consent in Settings at any time. Rewire then sends nothing more. Withdrawing does not affect requests already made.
  • Delete the app to delete everything it stores on your iPhone, except the Keychain entry.
  • Turn off permissions in iOS Settings: Screen Time (which also removes the shield), Reminders, Notifications and Camera. Karot works without Reminders, Notifications and Camera; Rewire's sheet check needs a photo.
  • Cancel a plan in iOS Settings, under your name, then Subscriptions. Deleting the app does not cancel a subscription.

The Keychain entry

The dates log described above lives in the iOS Keychain rather than in the app's storage. Keychain items often survive an app being deleted and reinstalled, and that is deliberate here: Karot is a commitment device, and the log is what lets it notice that it was deleted and reinstalled rather than forgetting.

  • It is best effort. Apple does not guarantee that Keychain items survive app deletion, and the app treats a missing log as a fresh install.
  • It is dates only, it stays on your iPhone, and no other app can read it. We cannot see it.
  • iOS has no button to delete one app's Keychain item. Erasing the iPhone (Settings, General, Transfer or Reset iPhone, Erase All Content and Settings) removes it. If you want it removed without that, email us and we will help.
