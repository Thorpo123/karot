Privacy Policy for Karot (Carrot)

Last updated: 24 October 2026

Karot is the App Store name of Carrot, an iOS app that shields social media on your own iPhone and unlocks short timed windows of access when you tick items off a daily to-do list. On your Home Screen it is called Carrot. This policy calls it Carrot.

The summary

There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third party code in the app.

Carrot itself works entirely on your phone. Only Rewire, the optional handwritten programme in the Carrot + Rewire plan, sends anything off the phone, and only after you agree in the app. Rewire sends your sheet photo, your goal, the first name you typed, your chosen affirmation, and your recent tied-task titles with whether you ticked them, through Carrot's own server, to OpenAI. Carrot's server passes them on and does not keep them. Your check-in answers never leave the phone.

Payments are handled entirely by Apple. Carrot never sees your card or your Apple Account details.

What Carrot stores on your device, and where

Carrot writes to three places, all on your phone.

1. The app's shared container

An App Group container (group.com.toby.carrot), shared between the app and its two Screen Time extensions. It holds:

  • Your daily list. The item titles you type, whether each is ticked, and the time of each tick.
  • The day's state. Credits earned and spent, whether a window is open and when it must end, and your settings (window length, and whether you have linked a Reminders list).
  • Your plan, as Apple reported it. Whether you have Carrot or Carrot + Rewire, and the date it runs to, so the shield can switch off by itself if the plan ends.
  • Your app selection, as opaque tokens. See "What Carrot cannot see" below.
  • A short history. A one line summary per day for the last 90 days, so the app can roll the day over correctly. It is written but never displayed.
  • Your Rewire programme, if you have started one: the goal, the name you typed, the chosen affirmation, day records with tied tasks and tick outcomes, and your weekly scores. The sheet photos are not stored, in this container or anywhere else.

This container is part of the app. Deleting Carrot deletes all of it.

2. The Keychain entry

One entry in the iOS Keychain holds a short factual log: the date you first installed Carrot, any later reinstall, days on which you used the once-a-day bypass, and any time Screen Time authorization was withdrawn. It is counts and dates. It contains no list items and no app names.

This entry behaves differently from everything else, and it is the one thing in this policy worth reading carefully. See "The Keychain entry and app deletion".

Carrot keeps no API key on your phone, and none is built into the app: the key for OpenAI lives on Carrot's server (see "Rewire" below). Any Keychain entry an earlier version stored a key under is deleted when the app launches; if the Keychain is unavailable at that moment the deletion is tried again on every later launch, until the entry is gone.

3. App settings

The app's own settings store remembers the date you agreed to Rewire's use of AI (so it does not ask again), the identifier of the App Attest key described below, and a few display facts (for example the day the last celebration played). Deleting the app deletes them.

Nothing else

Carrot creates no other files, uses no iCloud storage, and syncs to no other device. If you use Carrot on a second iPhone, the two know nothing about each other.

What Carrot cannot see

When you choose which apps to shield, iOS shows you Apple's own picker (FamilyActivityPicker). What the app receives back is a set of opaque tokens. These are meaningless identifiers that only the operating system can resolve.

Carrot cannot read them, cannot turn them back into app names, and therefore never knows which apps you chose. That is not a promise the app makes; it is how Apple built the API, and it is a deliberate privacy protection.

Carrot also does not receive, and cannot request, any record of your app usage history, browsing history, or how long you spend in any app. It knows only that an earned window's usage allowance has been reached, which iOS tells it as a single threshold event.

Plans and payment

Carrot is a free download with auto-renewable subscriptions sold through the App Store: Carrot, or Carrot + Rewire, monthly or yearly, each starting with a 7-day free trial. Apple takes the payment and holds your payment details. Carrot asks your iPhone (through Apple's StoreKit) which plan is active, and nothing else. When a plan ends, the shield switches off; nothing stays locked.

You manage or cancel a plan in iOS Settings, under your name, then Subscriptions. During a free trial, Carrot schedules one local reminder two days before the first payment.

Rewire

Rewire uses OpenAI, an AI company, to write your affirmation options and your daily tied task, and to check the photo of your handwritten sheet. The app asks for your agreement before it sends anything, and you can withdraw it in Settings at any time.

What is sent, and only when you use Rewire: your sheet photo, your goal, the first name you typed (if any), your chosen affirmation, and your tied-task titles from the last 7 days with whether you ticked them.

How it is sent. The app sends each request to Carrot's own server, a small relay run on Cloudflare Workers. The relay checks two things before it passes anything on: that the request comes from a genuine copy of Carrot on a genuine iPhone (Apple's App Attest), and that your Carrot + Rewire plan is active (the signed transaction Apple gave your iPhone). It then sends the request to OpenAI under Carrot's own OpenAI account and returns the answer. It does not keep the photo, the text or the answer, and it does not log them.

What the relay does keep:

  • for each install, the public half of its App Attest key and a counter, so the next request can be checked (no personal information);
  • for each plan, a count of today's requests, filed under a one-way hash of the plan's original transaction id, so the daily limit can apply; old counts are deleted automatically;
  • the day's total spend with OpenAI, as one number for all users.

What OpenAI does with it. Requests are made under Carrot's own OpenAI account, so OpenAI's API data-use terms apply. Under those terms OpenAI does not use API data to train its models, and may keep it for up to 30 days to check for abuse. The consumer ChatGPT terms do not apply.

The sheet photo is not stored on your phone, on Carrot's server, or anywhere else Carrot can reach. There is no background sending: requests happen only when you use Rewire.

Notifications

Carrot asks for notification permission the first time a reward window opens, or when you start a free trial. Its notifications are local and scheduled on your device: a reminder when an open window's time is up, a Sunday reminder to record your weekly score while Rewire is on, and one reminder two days before a free trial ends. There are no push notifications.

What Carrot does not do

Plainly, and in one place:

  • It does not collect personal information for itself. The only things that ever leave the phone are the Rewire items listed above, and only after you agree.
  • It does not use analytics, crash reporting SDKs, or advertising SDKs.
  • It does not track you across apps or websites, and it does not use the Advertising Identifier.
  • It does not contain any third party libraries or SDKs.
  • It does not create an account, and it does not know who you are.
  • It does not upload, back up, or sync your to-do items.
  • It does not monitor anybody else. Carrot is a tool for the owner of the phone it is installed on. There is no parent or guardian role, no second user, and no remote administration.
  • It does not read your app usage history or your browsing history.
  • It does not sell or share data with anyone. Rewire's requests go to OpenAI through Carrot's server, and nowhere else.

Children

Carrot is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

Deleting your data

Delete the app. That removes the shared container and everything in it: your lists, ticks, credits, settings, selection tokens, your Rewire programme, and the 90 day history. Nothing survives it except the Keychain entry described next. The relay's daily counts expire on their own; to have them removed sooner, email the address below.

To also remove the shield itself, revoke Screen Time access for Carrot in iOS Settings > Screen Time, before or after deleting the app.

To disconnect Reminders, revoke Reminders access in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Reminders. Reminders you already completed stay completed, as you would expect.

To stop a plan, cancel it in iOS Settings, under your name, then Subscriptions. Deleting the app does not cancel a subscription.

Reminders (optional)

If, and only if, you grant Reminders access, Carrot will:

  • read the titles and completion state of reminders in the one list you choose, so it can offer them when you plan your day;
  • mark a reminder complete when you tick the matching item in Carrot, and un-complete it if you un-tick that item.

All of this happens on your device through Apple's EventKit, between Carrot and the Reminders app. Nothing is copied off the phone. Carrot reads only the list you link, and it remembers the identifier and name of that list so it can find it again.

Reminders access is entirely optional. Carrot works fully without it, and you can revoke it at any time in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Reminders.

The Keychain entry and app deletion

This is the one exception, and it is deliberate, so it is documented rather than buried.

The install-and-bypass log lives in the iOS Keychain rather than in the app's container. On iOS, Keychain items written by an app are commonly observed to survive that app being deleted and reinstalled. That is the intended behaviour here: it is what lets Carrot say "installed on 2 August, reinstalled on 9 August" instead of quietly forgetting that deleting the app is a way around it. Carrot is a commitment device, and an amnesiac commitment device is not much of one.

Two honest qualifications:

  • It is best effort, not a guarantee. Apple does not contract that Keychain items survive app deletion, the behaviour has changed across iOS versions in the past, and it may change again. The app treats a missing log as a fresh install and carries on.
  • It is dates and counts only. Install date, reinstall dates, days on which the bypass was used, and Screen Time authorization changes. No list items, no app names, no usage data. It is never transmitted anywhere; the ledger is not among the things Rewire mode sends.

To remove it as well as the app: the entry is scoped to Carrot's own Keychain access group, so no other app and no file browser can reach it, and iOS provides no user-facing way to delete a single app's Keychain item. Your options are:

  1. Erase the iPhone (Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Erase All Content and Settings). This removes the Keychain in full. It is obviously drastic, and it is listed because it is the only complete answer.
  2. Ask. Email the address at the bottom of this page and a build that clears the entry on launch can be provided, or an in-app option added.
  3. Do nothing. It is a handful of dates on your own phone, readable by no app but Carrot and by no person but you, and it goes nowhere.
